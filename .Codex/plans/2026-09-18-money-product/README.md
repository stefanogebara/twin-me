# Money product review: useful decisions before more intelligence

Reviewed 18 September 2026. Baseline `69b55ec160f08bdc67b910e1538799b7728a9ee2`; changes on `codex/money-product-review`. Audience: one student and about ten friends. The owner's original working directory was preserved; implementation is in a separate worktree. “Fixed” below means implemented and locally tested in this change, not that every related risk is resolved. Release evidence is recorded at the end.

## Executive Summary

**Grade: C for a private beta; not yet a dependable financial predictor.** The ledger, source reconciliation and test infrastructure are substantial, but prediction confidence exceeds the evidence available and several correction paths disagree. The three biggest risks are incomplete/incorrect future-cost arithmetic, chat presenting associations as explanations, and corrections/deletions failing to reach every derived view or memory. The three best opportunities are a reliable daily decision screen, a single correction-to-recalculation path, and an account-aware ledger that distinguishes purchases, transfers and card repayments. A prediction “brain” already exists; adding another agent would increase uncertainty before fixing these foundations. This change improves spacing, puts an editable chat entry on Today, separates observed cards within bank accounts, and fixes several reproducible correctness and chat failures. Calendar and location should remain optional context until they demonstrate measurable incremental value. Invite friends as an explicitly experimental statement-based beta, with predictions labelled as estimates, rather than claiming validated financial guidance. The findings below separate code facts, observed results and product judgments.

## Audit Report

### Correctness and prediction — highest priority

**F1 — High, partly fixed: financial identity and user corrections were inconsistently applied.** Fact: commitment matching previously allowed an unrelated same-price series/payment to clear a named bill. Fact: the paginated analysis reader included `not_me` rows, and the common spending predicate did not reject them. Fact: actual spending applied personal shares while historical forecast baselines used gross amounts. Concrete consequence: rent could disappear, a rejected payment could still count, and a split dinner could inflate future spending. Fixed: identity plus amount matching (`projection.js:59`); rejected evidence excluded from calculations but retained in the review ledger (`transactionRepository.js:23`, `spending.js:29`); dissolved recurring series and flags removed after a verdict (`store.js:45`, `:177`); personal-share/exclusion rules applied to historical baselines (`projection.js:154`). Evidence: failing-first cases in `tests/api/services/money/productCorrectness.test.js:1` and real-Postgres correction/undo tests in `persistence.integration.test.js:192`. Remaining: verdict write and recurrence recomputation are multiple operations, not one transaction; a failure between them needs a durable retry/revision mechanism.

**F2 — High, open: recurring obligations are modelled by merchant, not by scheduled occurrence.** Fact: a merchant seen once this month is excluded from remaining recurring costs (`api/services/money/projection.js:99`); detection chooses one qualifying amount group per merchant (`recurring.js:93`). Consequence: weekly or fortnightly payments later in the same month can disappear, and two real subscriptions at one merchant can collapse into one. Judgment: this is a launch-priority arithmetic issue. Model series separately from merchants and enumerate every remaining due date, with cancellation/one-off overrides. `nextAfter` rolls old series forward (`recurring.js:71`), so do not assume silence means an active subscription indefinitely.

**F3 — High, open: missing history is treated as zero spending.** Fact: `dailyTotals` zero-fills the full requested interval (`projection.js:37`); the default history is 12 weeks and reported `history_days` is the filled length (`:151`, `:223`). Consequence: a short statement can look like 84 observed days and suppress the forecast. Judgment: an account's observed coverage must be explicit; “no transaction” is only zero inside a known-complete period. Use an abstention state outside that coverage, not invented quiet days.

**F4 — High, open: calendar costs may be counted on top of a baseline that already contains them.** Fact: event learning associates payments to nearby events (`calendar.js:143`) and expected event totals are added to the ordinary-spending forecast (`store.js:154`, `projection.js:106`, `:193`). The historical baseline does not subtract those event-associated costs. Consequence: a normal weekly dinner may be represented in both the baseline and the calendar addition. This is a structural double-counting risk, not a measured amount of overstatement for this owner. Until event effects are validated, show them as optional context or an explicit scenario; do not silently add full associated spend as an incremental causal effect.

**F5 — Medium, open: confidence is not validated accuracy.** Fact: next-payment confidence is a gap-model probability multiplied by a support heuristic (`brain.js:399`); a daily band becomes `trusted` after 60 scored days, irrespective of achieved coverage (`calibration.js:134`). Live observation on the existing production release: 6 expected charges/3 arrived, 5 scored daily allowances/1 held, 3 scored intervals/0 contained the actual, no scored previous month. These samples are far too small for a stable accuracy claim. They demonstrate that validation is early, not that the model is “0% accurate” universally. Require rolling-origin, horizon-specific evaluation against a weekday/recent-average baseline; assess interval coverage **and width**. See [Forecasting: Principles and Practice — time-series cross-validation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html) and [prediction intervals](https://otexts.com/fpp3/prediction-intervals.html). The new Today label says “Today's estimate” and exposes early calibration rather than implying bank-confirmed availability (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:598`).

**F6 — High, open: relationship is used as a substitute for payment purpose.** Fact: every transfer/Bizum to a friend, flatmate, family member or partner is excluded from spending (`spending.js:16`, `:29`). Consequence: paying a flatmate one's rent share or reimbursing a friend for dinner can disappear from consumption totals, even though the money funded a real cost. Judgment: keep separate fields for counterparty relationship, economic purpose and settlement linkage. Likewise, a credit-card bill is a transfer when its underlying purchases are already imported; when they are absent, show unclassified outflow rather than inventing those purchases. This change labels instruments but does not implement repayment reconciliation.

### Chat, architecture and reliability

**F7 — High, partly fixed: chat completion and persistence were not the same event.** Fact: a clean HTTP 200 EOF previously counted as success even without a `done` event; streaming history writes were fire-and-forget. Consequence: partial answers looked complete and a serverless invocation could end before history persisted. Fixed: explicit completion protocol, visible interruption/history failure, preserved partial text, and awaiting both local history writes (`moneyAPI.ts:352`, `chat.js:995`, `store.js:1178`, `MoneyChatPage.tsx:338`). Tests: `moneyStreamCompletion.test.ts:1`, `chatStream.test.js:1`, `chatStorageFailure.test.js:1`, browser interruption case. Remaining: two turn writes are not atomic/idempotent; non-streaming `answer` still catches save failures (`chat.js:806`); copying turns to the general twin remains asynchronous (`store.js:1190`).

**F8 — High, partly fixed: “saved” does not mean every screen or prediction has learned it.** Fact: Today/Month retained a 30-second snapshot and confirmed chat actions did not invalidate it; stored readings are separate rows, read without recomputing (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:110`, `store.js:523`). Fixed: account-scoped conversation remount, mutation revision/event, snapshot invalidation and immediate in-tab reload after a confirmed action (`moneyChanges.ts:1`, `MoneyV2Page.tsx:122`, `MoneyChatPage.tsx:215`). Browser evidence: Today → confirmed chat correction → Today updates within the cache window. Remaining: stored readings, general twin memory, other tabs and native clients do not share an acknowledged financial revision. A failed sub-request can still leave a mixed-age page (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:220`). Use one versioned snapshot and mark dependent views stale until rebuilt.

**F9 — High, open: number matching is not claim grounding.** Fact: `dropUngrounded` accepts an amount appearing anywhere in context, and `basisOf` selects lines sharing a number (`chat.js:1213`, `:1239`). Consequence: the correct amount can be attached to the wrong merchant, month or explanation and still pass. Judgment: “How it got there” must show a computed claim's entity, period, source IDs and calculation; model-generated reasoning is not verification. Keep free prose for context, but render monetary claims from typed, server-computed results. Test same-value/different-merchant and same-value/different-month adversarial cases.

**F10 — Medium, open: one-off knowledge is conversational, not a forecasting override.** Fact: `remember` stores a generic note (`chat.js:1297`), and context reads the last ten notes (`context.js:494`). There is no one-off action in the confirmed action dispatcher (`chat.js:1252`) that changes the baseline while retaining actual spend. Consequence: “this was a one-time trip” may improve wording but not the next prediction. Add a transaction/series-scoped exception with effective dates, user provenance and undo. Do not misuse `not_me`: a real one-off remains in historical actual spending.

**F11 — Medium, open: broad read paths and large modules make consistency expensive.** Fact: the shared page loads nine resources (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:220`), some reads recompute/store forecasts and recurrence (`store.js:45`, `:165`), and card inventory currently loads the complete bounded ledger (`instruments.js:24`). Fact: `MoneyV2Page.tsx`, `chat.js` and `store.js` each exceed 1,000 lines. Consequence: duplicate work, partial failures and separate freshness clocks; a large ledger can hit the analysis bound while listing accounts. Judgment: extract a small financial snapshot service and an indexed instrument query first. Do not split these files cosmetically into twenty wrappers or introduce microservices for eleven people.

### Calendar, location and privacy

**F12 — Medium, partly fixed: calendar capability is narrower than the UI suggests.** Facts: LMS events are deliberately excluded from direct cost learning (`calendar.js:127`); RRULE is recorded but occurrences are not expanded (`ics.js:107`, `:130`); background refresh starts from bank-feed users (`api/routes/cron-money-pull.js:48`). Consequence: generic recurring ICS events can be missing and statement-only friends are not covered by that background schedule. Live observation: Google and one Blackboard feed were connected, 183 events were read and one event-cost shape was learned; this does not establish forecast improvement. Fixed: a generic multi-day all-day assignment no longer becomes an “away/travel” interval (`covariates.js:1`, `calendar.js:1`; regression test). Keep academic deadlines/exam-week context; explicitly distinguish a read-only ICS feed from a full Canvas/Blackboard integration. Add recurrence/timezone/cancellation fixtures and an active-calendar refresh path before promising always-current schedules.

**F13 — High, open: forgetting a fact does not prove all copies were forgotten.** Facts: home coordinates are stored separately as hidden `home_point` (`home.js:415`); `deleteFact` removes a selected fact and its question record but not linked hidden rows or copied twin memories (`store.js:1166`); the bridge copies public facts into general memory (`twinBridge.js:124`). Consequence: forgetting the visible home or an incorrect note can leave sensitive or stale copies elsewhere. Judgment: deletion must follow provenance to derivatives, with tests verifying what can still be retrieved. Do not claim “remove a source and everything goes” without checking all copies (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:1079`).

**F14 — Medium, partly fixed: location copy promises classification that does not exist.** Facts: Money guesses an area from clusters of merchant locations (`home.js:88`, `:120`, `:373`), can persist coordinates after confirmation (`:415`), and uses study/work labels in context (`context.js:474`). No Money-specific continuous GPS collection was found; native config requests no location permission (`mobile/app.config.js:40`). Older unrelated web hooks do call geolocation (`src/hooks/useSunPosition.ts:147`, `useWeather.ts:59`), so a whole-platform “never uses location” claim would be false. Merchant location is not the customer's location, attendance, home or work purpose. Fixed: setup/save/chat copy now describes context rather than automatic work/commute attribution (`context.js:87`, `home.js:439`, `chat.js:473`). Recommendation: keep city/area optional, prefer coarse coordinates, expose deletion, and do not add background tracking for this beta.

**F15 — Medium, open: cross-user priors have a very small cohort floor.** Fact: pooled merchant priors require only two other users (`priors.js:27`) and are consumed during learning (`store.js:958`). This is not evidence of an exposed raw ledger, but it creates privacy/consent and representativeness questions for a ten-friend beta. Judgment: disable cross-person learning by default until there is explicit opt-in, a meaningful minimum cohort and measured benefit. The user's own history plus transparent conservative defaults are enough at this stage.

**F16 — Medium, partly fixed: visual hierarchy and account representation obscured the useful task.** Observation: before changes, the 1,200px desktop Today capture was 1,961px tall, with a 360px globe and the useful headline starting around y=503. Fixed: 40px section rhythm, 20px phone gutters, tighter related-copy gaps, a readable tappable figure and a pill entry to the existing chat (`src/styles/money-v2.css:1`, `HomeAsk.tsx:1`, `MoneyV2Page.tsx:598`). Accounts now contain their observed card suffixes and explicit unknown/credit/debit choices (`BankAccounts.tsx:1`, `instruments.js:1`). Ownership and observed-card existence are checked server-side (`instruments.js:34`) and tested in Postgres. Remaining: bank UI enumerates a fixed bank list and the account endpoint excludes manual statement accounts (`MoneyV2Page.tsx:1085`, `store.js:253`); build a unified account inventory next. A suffix is evidence of a card reference, not a guaranteed globally unique physical card ID.

**Testing/operations.** Strength: real persistence and browser gates exist. Limitation: browser fixtures test integration behavior with mocked API responses, not model answer quality or actual bank authorization (`tests/e2e/money-beta-fixture.ts:30`). Native iOS/Android behavior and causal/prediction quality need separate tests. CI still allows existing 73 ESLint errors and 110 TypeScript errors while rejecting growth (`scripts/ci/baselines.json:1`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml:67`); these are debt, not a clean type check. One unrelated timing-sensitive ingestion test failed under concurrent full-suite/build load and passed on isolated rerun; record it rather than silently calling the first run green. Dependencies/lockfiles were not changed; no current-CVE or license-cleanliness claim is made in this focused pass.

**Documentation.** `README.md:1` and parts of the CI header (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:10`) describe older product/gate behavior. Several Money comments describe a monolithic store or stale design sizes although modules and styling have moved. ### Strengths to preserve

- Evidence and canonical payments are separate; reconciliation and owner isolation have executable Postgres tests (`persistence.integration.test.js:30`).
- The financial engine is mostly ordinary testable code, not an LLM calculating balances (`projection.js:74`, `recurring.js:78`).
- Chat actions are proposed, confirmed and server-revalidated (`chat.js:297`, `:1252`).
- Calendar feed secrets are encrypted and omitted from public feed objects; fetches have network guards (`calendar.js:391`, `:413`, `:491`).
- Unknown instrument type remains unknown; the new card-label endpoint cannot write another owner's account (`instruments.js:34`).
- Failure, empty data and uncertainty already have dedicated paths; the right next step is consistency, not a rewrite.

### Five themes and target states

1. **One financial meaning.** Actual spending, cash movement, personal consumption, committed bills and predicted discretionary spending are different quantities. Give each a typed definition and preserve account/currency/payment identity. Done: correction fixtures produce the same result in Today, Month, Plan and chat; no duplicate credit-card repayment or missing weekly obligation.
2. **Learning that changes a decision.** Use the existing engine as a small evidence system: observations → canonical events/instruments → confirmed facts/overrides → hypotheses → evaluated forecasts. Each derived claim carries source IDs, model/rule version, timestamp and scope. Done: a one-off action changes the baseline without erasing actual spending, and the UI states exactly what changed.
3. **Earn uncertainty.** Track observed coverage, abstain when insufficient, and compare every model against a simple baseline using past-only training. Done: horizon-specific error/coverage reports, prediction intervals labelled with sample count, no “trusted” badge based only on elapsed days. A minimum sample is necessary but not sufficient; the release threshold must include achieved performance and interval usefulness.
4. **Confirmed changes propagate and can be undone.** A correction returns a financial revision; each dependent view either shows that revision or says it is updating. Deletion follows copied data. Done: persistence, failed-rebuild, reload, multi-tab and cross-client tests; no success message for an uncommitted change.
5. **Fewer surfaces, clearer value.** Each screen helps answer “what happened?”, “what is coming?”, or “what should I correct?”. Done: five friends can find a payment, explain the daily estimate and correct a split without guidance; their observed failures determine the next UI change.

### What a useful “brain” should do

Do not create another autonomous LLM service. Keep Postgres and the existing pure functions. Add explicit records for **instrument identity**, **transaction purpose**, **series occurrences**, **coverage intervals**, and **user overrides**. The LLM can propose an interpretation; deterministic code validates/applies it and computes money. An explanation should say “three charges, about 30 days apart; you confirmed this is monthly,” rather than “I know your habits.”

For a one-off: retain the payment in actuals, attach an exception to its transaction or event, exclude only the appropriate discretionary baseline contribution, retain it as an explained anomaly and allow undo. For recurrence: use cadence, amount variation, merchant/account identity, missed expected occurrences, cancellation and explicit confirmation. Three similar payments are a candidate pattern, not a permanent contract.

### Correlation, cause and useful context

A restaurant charge during exam week establishes timing. It does not establish that exams caused the charge: weekday, friends, income timing and missing accounts can explain both. A user saying “I bought dinner because I was studying late” is a **reported reason**, with user provenance. A causal estimate needs a defined intervention, outcome and defensible assumptions about confounding; see [Hernán and Robins, Causal Inference: What If](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook). This beta should offer descriptive comparisons and optional scenarios, not causal declarations. Later, an opt-in personal experiment can compare a specific habit across matched days, while honestly reporting small samples and remaining confounding.

**ICS value:** a read-only feed can surface exam/deadline weeks, distinguish a changed routine from a permanent habit and show a known event next to a spending scenario. It does not read grades, prove attendance, or synchronize all LMS content. Keep only if users understand and act on the added context; compare forecast error with/without calendar before letting it alter the base estimate. The existing exclusion of academic events from direct costs is sensible.

**Location value:** merchant identity/city can improve categories and evidence; user-confirmed home/study/work context can make questions more relevant. It should not imply surveillance. Keep category (“restaurant”), economic purpose (“work meal”), funding instrument (“credit card”) and reimbursement status as separate axes. Location alone cannot assign any of the last three. Default to no background location collection and make saved area/point retention visible and removable.

**Trade-offs.** Do not add a graph database, multi-agent finance manager, real-time location tracking, elaborate causal model, enterprise event bus or whole-platform redesign. Do not bulk-upgrade unrelated dependencies or rewrite legacy soul features in this change. Keep Plan's route until navigation can be simplified with evidence. Spending time on correct weekly bills and reliable undo has much higher payoff than richer artwork or another prediction model.

### Implementation sketches for the top three remaining tasks

**T2 — correction/deletion revision.** Start with an `apply_money_action` transaction that checks owner, action ID and expected revision, writes the structured override plus an audit record, and increments one user's revision. Return that revision with the confirmed effect. A small durable rebuild record, processed within existing request/cron budgets, updates derived views; readers reject older revisions. Add source-fact IDs to copied memories and delete/tombstone by provenance. Gotchas: repeated clicks, partial rebuilds, action conflicts, existing memories lacking provenance, and a saved user turn whose assistant turn failed. Split delivery into atomic actions, view propagation, and deletion migration; do not put them into one unreviewable change.

**T3 — obligations/payment meaning.** Introduce a series ID keyed by account/merchant/amount cluster, then generate dated occurrences rather than checking “seen this merchant this month.” Classify cash movement separately from personal expense. Link card settlement to the owned card account and already imported purchases; never infer that linkage from equal amount alone. Add transaction-specific one-off/own-share overrides and cancellation dates, preserving original evidence. Gotchas: mixed currencies, refunds, pending-to-booked changes, same-price purchases, missing card statements, shared rent, and two services billed by one merchant.

**T4 — forecast coverage/evaluation.** Record the coverage supplied by a bank read or statement; unknown periods remain unknown. Build past-only dated snapshots from T1 and synthetic/consented anonymized histories. Compare simple weekday/recent-average forecasts against the existing model by horizon; measure point error and interval coverage/width. Remove additive calendar effects until a model measures their incremental contribution; keep scenario UI separate. Gotchas: inferring completeness from first transaction, leakage from revised facts, selecting only successful predictions, and calling an extremely wide interval “accurate.”
